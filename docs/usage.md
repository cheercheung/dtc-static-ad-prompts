# Using the static ad templates

[Return to the library](../README.md)

This guide explains how to turn a template into a campaign-specific working
prompt. The 40 template bodies are preserved in the library. Work on a copy;
keep the canonical prompt text unchanged when applying your brand details.
The templates describe creative formats. They do not establish product facts,
customer results, media coverage, model quality, or advertising performance.

## 1. Choose the creative job

| Creative job | Category | What to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| State a message or offer | Messaging & Offers | Headline, supporting copy, actual offer terms |
| Present customer feedback | Reviews & Social Proof | Genuine quotes, ratings, counts, and permitted identifying details |
| Explain features or numbers | Benefits & Data | Verified benefits, units, ingredient or material facts |
| Show a comparison or change | Comparisons & Transformations | Comparable facts or documented before/after material |
| Use editorial or native formats | Editorial & Native Formats | Approved story, opinion, educational copy, and format-specific assets |
| Show products in context | Lifestyle & Product Displays | Product references, variants or bundle information, scene direction |

Use case numbers to keep track of selections. Categories group related creative
jobs; they do not rank the templates or claim that a format will convert better.
Case 20 includes a music-oriented scene example. Retain the template body, then
fill its scene and story inputs with a relevant DTC product context in your
working copy. Case 39 is a problem-led hook without a visible product.

## 2. Prepare Brand DNA

Record the brand's confirmed identity and proposed visual choices separately.
When reading a website, use information actually present on its pages. A color
or style estimate is a design proposal for review, not a confirmed brand rule.
If the site cannot be read, ask for a manual brief instead of guessing facts.

Copy this form into your own campaign workspace:

```
Brand name:
Audience and positioning:
Voice (five specific adjectives):
Approved example of brand copy:
Lead / support / accent / background colors, with hex values:
Confirmed colors vs proposed estimates:
Headline and body typography styles:
Photography: lighting, framing, setting, surfaces, color grade:
Packaging and label details that must remain recognizable:
Required visual elements:
Visual elements to avoid:
```

## 3. Prepare Product DNA

Keep claims tied to a product page, packaging, a verified internal fact sheet,
or another record you can check. Write down where each factual input came from
in your private working brief. An empty field means more information is needed.
Do not turn an estimate or an unreviewed generated profile into a product fact.

```
Product name and category:
Physical form, material, label, and packaging:
Sizes / flavors / colors / variants:
Approved description and tagline:
Verified benefits:
Ingredients / materials:
Confirmed exclusions:
Verified numbers, units, and supporting records:
Comparison basis and relevant alternatives:
Available product reference images:
```

## 4. Review a campaign brief before filling the prompt

For every selected case, list all copy a viewer would read. Brand voice guides
wording; verified Product DNA supplies factual content. Match word counts,
number of callouts, and other formatting requirements stated in that prompt.
Approve the full brief before producing the final working prompt.

```
Selected case number and template title:
Objective and target audience:
Headline / subhead:
Offer, price, dates, and actual terms:
Benefits / ingredients / materials:
Statistics and units:
Review quote, name, rating, and count, when applicable:
Press quote or publication identity, when applicable:
Comparison strengths / weaknesses, when applicable:
Required scene, people, and reference assets:
Aspect ratio from the template body:
Missing inputs to request:
Approved campaign copy:
```

Missing review counts, star ratings, customer names, press coverage, numerical
claims, or competitor facts stay unresolved until genuine material is supplied.
A warning symbol beside an invented number does not make it verified. Do not
invent a testimonial or media endorsement to complete a template. Review-card,
social-comment, and news-style templates describe a visual format; do not pass
fabricated UI, customers, or coverage off as real evidence. If a concept mockup
uses fictional content, keep that status explicit in the resulting concept.

## 5. Fill a separate working copy

Replace all bracketed inputs, including nested inputs such as a brand name
inside a headline. Example wording introduced by “like” or “e.g.” illustrates a
slot; it is not a fact to carry into another brand's ad. Keep all approved copy
consistent with the brief and introduce no new factual values while filling.

Resolve colors and text contrast explicitly. Prefer dark text on light
backgrounds and light text on dark backgrounds where the layout permits it.
Keep the chosen typography, packaging, and product geometry consistent with
Brand DNA and the actual references. A prompt cannot guarantee that an image
tool will preserve a logo, label, face, or product exactly; inspect the result.

## 6. Supply references that fit the template

A clear product photo is useful when the template shows a product: use a sharp,
well-lit image with readable packaging. Additional brand references can help
establish colors and style. Supply enough information for everything requested;
one image of one SKU does not establish a product range, bundle, or person.

| Case | Requirement or exception |
|---|---|
| 11 | The body permits 1:1 or 3:4. Choose one for the working prompt and the image tool. |
| 12 | Shows three color variants. Provide references or confirmed descriptions for the actual variants. |
| 14 | Remains a bundle/open-box template with multiple products. Supply the real bundle and packaging details. |
| 20 | Says “No product shot.” Use the permitted tone reference without forcing a product into the scene. |
| 29 | Specifies no visible product, brand logo, or CTA. Preserve that scene logic when filling the copy. |
| 39 | Specifies no product, logo, branding, or CTA. The problem visual and hook carry the concept. |

Do not prepend a universal instruction that every ad must show the reference
product. That conflicts with Cases 20, 29, and 39. The one-SKU rule is also not
applied to Case 14. Case 18 is the lifestyle-flatlay stat-radial format; Case 13
is its product-hero counterpart.

## 7. Use the aspect ratio in the prompt body

Each category page displays the ratio taken from the template's terminal ratio
instruction. The body takes precedence over a separate menu label. Case 11
permits 1:1 or 3:4; choose one before generating. Case 34 is 1:1: the values
10:45 and 10:44 inside its UI description are times, not aspect ratios.

Use an image tool that accepts the required references and the chosen format.
If it has an aspect-ratio setting, set it consistently with the working prompt.
Generate one selected concept, review it, and repeat or revise as needed. This
text edition does not include generated examples or model-effect comparisons.

## 8. Review the generated ad

Check the exact on-image copy, product shape and label, variant count, scene,
layout, contrast, aspect ratio, and factual claims against the approved brief.
Check whether reviews, people, press, or social UI imply evidence you do not
have. Correct unsupported content in the working concept before using it.

Keep your brief, filled prompt, reference set, and chosen output together in
your own campaign workspace. Compare only observed campaign results when
assessing performance; the existence of a template is not a result.
